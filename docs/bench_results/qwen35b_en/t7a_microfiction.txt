After her fourteenth rebirth, she decided not to save him again. The time loop had grown tedious, a repetitive dance of panic, sacrifice, and hollow gratitude. She watched from the shadows as the car skidded on the rain-slicked road, its headlights cutting through the fog like desperate eyes. This time, she didn’t scream. She didn’t run. She simply let the physics of momentum take their inevitable course.

The crash was loud, final, and strangely peaceful. As the silence settled, she checked her pocket watch. The numbers didn’t reset. Instead, they advanced to 8:01 AM.

A knock echoed at her door. "Delivery for Elena," a voice called. She opened it to find not her lover, but a man in a white coat holding a clipboard. "Excellent work," he said, tapping a box. "The simulation is complete. You’ve finally broken the attachment algorithm. Welcome to the real world."
