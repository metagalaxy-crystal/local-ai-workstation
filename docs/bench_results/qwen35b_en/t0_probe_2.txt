The Silk Road was not a single paved road, but a vast, interconnected network of trade routes linking the East and West, stretching from Chang’an (modern-day Xi’an) in China to the Mediterranean Sea. Active primarily from the 2nd century BCE to the 14th century CE, it facilitated not only the exchange of goods but also profound cultural, religious, and technological diffusion.

### I. Main Goods Traded

The trade was characterized by high-value, low-volume goods that could withstand long, arduous journeys across deserts, mountains, and steppes.

#### A. From East to West (China, India, Southeast Asia → Middle East, Europe, Africa)

1. **Silk**:  
   - The namesake commodity. Chinese silk was highly prized in Rome, Persia, and Byzantium for its luxury, lightness, and status symbolism.  
   - Initially a closely guarded Chinese secret, silk production eventually spread to Byzantium in the 6th century CE.
