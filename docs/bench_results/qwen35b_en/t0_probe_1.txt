The Silk Road was not a single paved highway, but a vast, interconnected network of trade routes linking the East (primarily China and India) with the West (the Mediterranean, Persia, and Europe). Active roughly from the 2nd century BCE to the 15th century CE, it facilitated not only the movement of goods but also the flow of ideas, religions, technologies, and cultures across Eurasia.

Below is a detailed description of the main goods traded and the profound cultural exchanges enabled by this network.

---

### I. Main Goods Traded

While silk is the namesake of the route, the trade was far more diverse. Goods were often carried in segments by different merchant groups, as no single trader typically journeyed the entire length from Chang’an to Rome.

#### A. From East to West (China, India, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia)

1. **Silk**:
   - The most iconic commodity. Chinese silk was highly prized in Rome and Persia for its texture, luster, and status-symbol value.
   - Silk was used not only for clothing but also as currency, diplomatic gifts, and even a form of taxation in some periods.
