After her fourteenth rebirth, she decided not to save him again.

She moved through lifetimes like a shadow through mirrors: a thief in snow, a queen in ash, a child beneath a dying star. Each time, he appeared—broken, bleeding, reaching across impossible distances. She saved him. She always saved him. Until the present, where he was a man sitting at her kitchen table, stirring cold tea.

“You remember me?” he asked.

She froze. No curse. No prophecy. Only ordinary rain against the window.

“I remember every version,” she whispered. “Every time I failed to stop you from dying.”

He smiled, weary and kind. “Then why save me now?”

She looked at her hands—ancient, trembling. The twist unfolded like a closing door: she had never been saving *him*. Across fourteen lives, she had been saving *themselves*, each rebirth a rescue from the man she was destined to become. He was the anchor. Her refusal to save him again was the final choice to break the cycle.

She set down the tea. “No more saving,” she said.

Outside, the rain stopped. For the first time, time held its breath.
