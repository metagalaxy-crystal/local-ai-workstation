The Silk Road—a vast network of overland and maritime trade routes connecting East Asia (particularly China), Central Asia, the Middle East, South Asia, and Europe—was far more than a conduit for silk. Over centuries (roughly from the 2nd century BCE to the 15th century CE), it facilitated the exchange of goods, ideas, technologies, religions, and artistic traditions across continents. Below is a detailed description of the main goods traded and the cultural exchanges enabled by this network.

---

## 📦 Main Goods Traded Along the Silk Road

### **1. Textiles & Luxury Fabrics**
- **Silk**: The route’s namesake—Chinese silk was highly prized in Rome, Persia, and beyond for its rarity, fineness, and sheen. It was traded not only as fabric but also embroidered goods and ceremonial items.
- **Other Textiles**: Wool, linen, cotton, and damask fabrics moved across regions, with local styles influencing each other.
